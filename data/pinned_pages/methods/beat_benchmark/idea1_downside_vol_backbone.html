One-sentence version. Keep Book 2 exactly as it is, but measure the risk that drives the vol-target scaling with downside volatility (the size of the bad days only) instead of total volatility. The 2021 paper behind this says the switch adds return timing, not just a volatility cut, which is the axis Book 2 is missing. Target benchmark: the vol-target backbone (primary null), with Book 2 shown beside it. Its CAGR edge over the static core at 10 bp is negative (−0.26 pp on the 68-month window, in-sample), so it does not qualify for the reserved 2008–16 holdout.

1. The idea in plain language

Book 2's edge so far is a smoother ride, not more return. Its Sharpe gain over the backbone comes from cutting volatility (NW t of the return gap -0.80 over 68 months). Wang & Yan (2021) found, across 9 equity factors and 94 anomaly portfolios, that scaling by downside volatility beats scaling by total volatility in direct Sharpe comparisons and in real-time strategies. Their decomposition says most of the gain is return timing: high downside volatility tends to come before weak returns. Barroso & Detzel (2021) add the piece most relevant to us: after trading costs, the volatility-managed market portfolio is the case that survives, and our core is market-like (70/20/10 S&P 500 / Nasdaq-100 / small-cap). Cederburg et al. (2020) are the warning: across 103 strategies, real-time versions often lose to the unmanaged portfolio. So the bar has to be a real-time comparison against the backbone, not a spanning-regression alpha.

2. The math

Backbone today (registry vol_target_option_a_uncond):
  σ̂_t  = √252 · sd(r_core, last 63 trading days to month-end t)
  σ*_t = √252 · sd(r_core, all days to t)            (expanding)
  f_t  = clip(σ*_t / σ̂_t, 0.25, 1)                    (scale down only)
  R_{t+1} = f_t·R_core,t+1 + (1 − f_t)·R_BIL,t+1 − 5bp·|f_t − f_{t−1}|
Book 2:  f̃_t = f_t · g_t,  g_t ∈ {0.5, 1} from the skew / CVaR5 gate

The change is one line, the risk estimate:

σ̂ᴰ_t  = √252 · √( (2/63) · Σ_(s=t−62..t) min(r_s, 0)² )
σ*ᴰ_t = same estimator, expanding over all days to t
fᴰ_t  = clip(σ*ᴰ_t / σ̂ᴰ_t, 0.25, 1);   Book 2 variant: f̃ᴰ_t = fᴰ_t · g_t (gate unchanged)

The factor 2 makes σᴰ equal σ when returns are symmetric, so the 0.25 floor, the cap at 1 and the target keep their meaning. Nothing else changes, and there are no new knobs.

Why it could add return, not just cut risk

For a scaled strategy, E[f_t R_t+1] = E[f]·E[R] + Cov(f_t, μ_t), where μ_t is the conditional mean. Total-volatility scaling earns its keep through the variance term: it is cheap insurance when volatility clusters. It adds return only if Cov(f, μ) > 0. Wang & Yan's evidence is that downside volatility predicts μ negatively more reliably than total volatility does, so fᴰ should have a more positive Cov(fᴰ, μ). If that is false on our panel, fᴰ ≈ f and the test comes out flat. In our data the two scalings are nearly the same series (correlation 0.9982 in the illustration below), which is why the comparison is cheap to run and has unusually high power.

5. Honest odds and how it fails

Expected edge: Sharpe +0.00 to +0.03 over the backbone, with no material change to drawdown, and CAGR still below the static core. Probability of a meaningful gated PASS against the backbone (point gate plus a significant paired test, DSR reported) on 24–60 forward months: about 10–15%. That assumes about a 50% prior that a true edge of 0.015–0.03 exists. A point-estimate-only pass, like Book 2's admission, is close to a coin flip (50–75%), and it would not mean much on its own. It does not beat the static core on CAGR.
  • No return timing on a market portfolio. Wang & Yan's evidence is mostly factor and anomaly long-short portfolios. On a long-only market core, downside and total volatility may carry the same information. Then fᴰ ≈ f and the result is flat.
  • Noisier estimator. Downside variance uses about half the days, so it is noisier at 63 days. It could add turnover or whipsaw in sharp V-shaped rebounds (2020).
  • Window dependence. The whole vol-target family trailed the core over 2018-12 to 2026-09 and led it over 2021-02 to 2026-09. A short forward test can land on either side.
  • Interaction with the gate. On Book 2, the skew/CVaR gate already reacts to left-tail behaviour, so part of any downside-vol gain may already be captured.

6. What a gate would look like

  • Candidate: one frozen spec, VT_option_a_L63_downside_sigexpanding_fmax1p0 (and the Book 2 version with the gate unchanged). No lookback or floor grid.
  • Primary null: the unconditional vol-target backbone, on the same months, costs and core. Secondary: Book 2 and the static core.
  • Pass rule (proposal): net Sharpe ex-BIL above the backbone (point estimate), with a paired one-sided p < 0.05 (HAC, meaning Newey–West-style standard errors that allow for changing volatility and month-to-month correlation, and circular block bootstrap), and MaxDD no more than 2 pp worse. NW t of the return gap reported.
  • Window: forward only, from the first complete month after the registry row is written. 2021-02 to 2026-09 has been seen and cannot be reused.
  • Panel: load_monthly_panel(path, complete_months_only=True), which drops a partial final month. write_gate_results records complete_months_only, dropped_partial_month and source_asof. Daily prices for the volatility estimate end on the last trading day of the decision month.
  • Composition tripwire: composition_tripwire(weights, universe, method=…, primary_null=…) makes the run VOID if the average cash_like + short_duration + near_cash share (near_cash = FTSL, SRLN) is above 50% for the method or its primary null. The overlay parks the unused exposure in BIL, which is cash_like, so the share is real and must be printed: about 9% in the illustration. Then final_gate_label(mechanical, composition) sets the label and composition_report_lines(result) goes into the report.
  • Trial count carried forward: a new overlay inherits Book 2's overlay registry (72 skew-grid specs) plus the backbone (1), so N = 73, and this shortlist adds 3, giving about 120 project-wide (hub: about 117 before this). The registry row is written before any run.
  • DSR reported (Bailey & López de Prado form used in the repo, registry cross-trial variance V = 0.014061), at the candidate's own N = 1, at N = 3 (this shortlist), at N = 73 and at N ≈ 120, with realised skew and kurtosis. As with Book 2, DSR is reported, not the admission gate, because no equity-core overlay can reach the N = 73 hurdle (see the feasibility section).
  • Costs: 5 bp one-way on |Δf| (exposure changes), 10 bp sensitivity. Leakage tests: every σ̂ and signal is dated at or before the decision month-end; returns are for the next month.

Holdout: reserved

The 2008-01 to 2016-10 window stays reserved. An idea qualifies for a holdout spend only if its CAGR edge over the static core at 10 bp is positive on data we are allowed to read. Any spend then needs its own pre-registration signed off by both CIO and Chief of Staff, plus Jared's choice, with the prior-use disclosures of Addendum 3 §1.2. Idea 1 does not meet the qualification test: its CAGR edge over the core at 10 bp is −2.37 pp over 2018-12 to 2026-09 and −0.26 pp over 2021-02 to 2026-09. So no holdout spend is proposed. Separately, any future spend would first need an audit confirming that no earlier registry ran a vol-scaled core over 2008–16. That window is already "lightly used" by FT-MED, RR-ERC, Regime-dual and the Spectral RP sleeve.
