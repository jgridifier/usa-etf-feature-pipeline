One-sentence version. Draw the line of fixed mixes between Book 1 (the static core) and Book 2, from w = 0 (all core) to w = 1 (all Book 2), and read off the mix that matches how deep a drawdown Jared is willing to sit through. No trial is charged. Nothing is estimated or selected from returns; the table simply shows two live books that already exist.

1. The idea in plain language

Jared's default is the static core. Book 2 beats it on the risk axis (Sharpe ex-BIL 0.99 vs 0.77, MaxDD −10.1% vs −25.6%, 2021-02 to 2026-09) but trails it on CAGR (13.9% vs 15.0%). Any fixed mix of the two sits between them. The frontier below makes that trade-off visible. Post-2020 work explains why the mix should be fixed by preference rather than estimated. Wang & Yan (2021) find that fixed-weight combinations of managed and unmanaged portfolios fix much of the real-time weakness of volatility management. Cederburg et al. (2020) trace that weakness to unstable estimated combination weights. Kan, Wang & Zhou (2022) show how estimation risk eats an estimated optimal mix in short samples. DeMiguel, Martín-Utrera & Uppal (2024) give the combination view of volatility management.

2. The math

R_w = w·R_Book2 + (1 − w)·R_core,    w ∈ [0, 1]
Because Book 2 = f̃_t·core + (1 − f̃_t)·BIL:
  R_w = (1 − w(1 − f̃_t))·R_core + w(1 − f̃_t)·R_BIL
  ⇒ one exposure e_t = 1 − w(1 − f̃_t); one-way turnover = w × Book 2's

So every blend is Book 2's own signal at strength w. Costs scale with w: Book 2's live one-way turnover is 42%/yr, so the blend's is w × 42%. The 10 bp column doubles Book 2's recorded 5 bp cost. The live core file carries no rebalancing cost, so the core is shown uncharged.

Why w is chosen by preference, not estimated

The Sharpe-maximising mix of two risky positions is w* ∝ Σ⁻¹(μ − r_f). It depends on the mean gap between Book 2 and the core, and over the same 68 months that gap is not identified (NW t -0.69 for Book 2 vs core). An estimated w* would mostly fit noise. That is the KWZ estimation-risk problem in its simplest form, and Wang & Yan's fixed-weight remedy. So w is set from drawdown tolerance, which is a statement about Jared, not about the data.

4. Status: no trial charged

  • The frontier reads two existing live books. Nothing is selected from returns, so no trial is added and the project count is unchanged.
  • It becomes a trial only if a specific w is pre-registered and run forward. It is then held to the Book rule as written: one-way turnover ≤ 100%/yr (it passes at any w) and CAGR above the core at 10 bp costs. On its own numbers it is unlikely to clear that second test, since every w > 0 gives up CAGR on the 68-month window.
  • If pre-registered, the gate conventions apply: load_monthly_panel(path, complete_months_only=True); composition_tripwire + final_gate_label + composition_report_lines (VOID if average cash_like + short_duration + near_cash > 50%, near_cash = FTSL, SRLN; the BIL share here is w × 24%); trial count carried forward (N = 73 for the overlay line); DSR reported.
  • Holdout: not applicable. Book 2's gate has never been run on 2008–16, and the window stays reserved.

5. How it can mislead

  • It is not new information. Every point inherits Book 2's edge and Book 2's uncertainty. If Book 2's risk edge fades forward, the whole line moves toward the core.
  • CAGR is structurally behind in rising markets. When the gate or the scaling binds during a rally, every blend lags. A blend can only beat the core on CAGR in windows with a real drawdown.
  • One window. The 68 months include one large drawdown (2022). The shape of the line is specific to that path.
