One-sentence version. Replace the backbone's "last 63 days" volatility with a forecast of next month's volatility from a heterogeneous-autoregressive (HAR) regression on daily, weekly and monthly squared returns, so the scaling reacts faster when risk rises and recovers faster when it falls. Target benchmark: the vol-target backbone.

1. The idea in plain language

The backbone scales down by the ratio of long-run to recent volatility over a 63-day window. That window is slow: it keeps the book de-risked for weeks after a shock has passed, the pattern Bongaerts, Kang & van Dijk (2020) describe as making conventional volatility targeting worse in sharp rebounds. A HAR forecast weights yesterday, last week and last month separately, so it tracks volatility clustering more closely. Christensen, Siggaard & Veliyev (2023) is a recent large comparison in which HAR models are the standard benchmark and machine-learning forecasts beat them only modestly. We stay with classical HAR (statistical, no large models) because its three coefficients are cheap to estimate on our sample.

2. The math

Backbone today (registry vol_target_option_a_uncond):
  σ̂_t  = √252 · sd(r_core, last 63 trading days to month-end t)
  σ*_t = √252 · sd(r_core, all days to t)            (expanding)
  f_t  = clip(σ*_t / σ̂_t, 0.25, 1)                    (scale down only)
  R_{t+1} = f_t·R_core,t+1 + (1 − f_t)·R_BIL,t+1 − 5bp·|f_t − f_{t−1}|
Book 2:  f̃_t = f_t · g_t,  g_t ∈ {0.5, 1} from the skew / CVaR5 gate
Daily proxy: x_d = r_d²   (no intraday data on this panel)
Target:   y_t = (1/21)·Σ_(k=1..21) x_(t+k)          (next-month mean)
HAR:      y_t = b0 + b1·x_t + b2·mean(x_(t−4..t)) + b3·mean(x_(t−21..t)) + e_t
OLS on an expanding window ending at month-end t (only y's fully observed by t)
σ̂ᴴ_t = √(252 · max(ŷ_t, 0.25·mean22(x)));   fᴴ_t = clip(σ*_t / σ̂ᴴ_t, 0.25, 1)

The floor guards against a negative OLS forecast. No other knobs.

5. Honest odds and how it fails

Expected edge: Sharpe −0.04 to +0.08 vs the backbone, sign uncertain, with drawdown likely worse. Probability of a meaningful gated PASS: about 5–10%. It is ranked last because its in-sample sign flips across windows and it gives up some of the drawdown control that Book 2 was admitted for.
  • Noisy proxy. Without intraday data, daily r² is a poor realized-variance proxy, and the HAR literature's gains assume better measures.
  • Faster re-risking cuts both ways. It helps in V-shaped rebounds (2020) and hurts in drawn-out sell-offs with relief rallies (2022).
  • Drawdown is the axis we have evidence on. A rule that raises Sharpe by holding more equity can still lose the risk-path comparison.

6. What a gate would look like

  • Candidate: one frozen spec (HAR on daily r², expanding OLS, floor as above). Primary null: the backbone. Pass rule: as Idea 1 (Sharpe above the backbone with paired p < 0.05, MaxDD no more than 2 pp worse), forward only.
  • Panel: load_monthly_panel(path, complete_months_only=True), which drops a partial final month. write_gate_results records complete_months_only, dropped_partial_month and source_asof. Daily prices for the volatility estimate end on the last trading day of the decision month.
  • Composition tripwire: composition_tripwire(weights, universe, method=…, primary_null=…) makes the run VOID if the average cash_like + short_duration + near_cash share (near_cash = FTSL, SRLN) is above 50% for the method or its primary null. The overlay parks the unused exposure in BIL, which is cash_like, so the share is real and must be printed: about 5% in the illustration. Then final_gate_label(mechanical, composition) sets the label and composition_report_lines(result) goes into the report.
  • Trial count carried forward: a new overlay inherits Book 2's overlay registry (72 skew-grid specs) plus the backbone (1), so N = 73, and this shortlist adds 3, giving about 120 project-wide (hub: about 117 before this). The registry row is written before any run.
  • DSR reported (Bailey & López de Prado form used in the repo, registry cross-trial variance V = 0.014061), at the candidate's own N = 1, at N = 3 (this shortlist), at N = 73 and at N ≈ 120, with realised skew and kurtosis. As with Book 2, DSR is reported, not the admission gate, because no equity-core overlay can reach the N = 73 hurdle (see the feasibility section).
  • Costs: 5 bp one-way on |Δf| (exposure changes), 10 bp sensitivity. Leakage tests: every σ̂ and signal is dated at or before the decision month-end; returns are for the next month.

Holdout: reserved. The 2008-01 to 2016-10 window stays reserved. An idea qualifies for a holdout spend only if its CAGR edge over the static core at 10 bp is positive on data we are allowed to read. Any spend then needs its own pre-registration signed off by both CIO and Chief of Staff, plus Jared's choice, with the prior-use disclosures of Addendum 3 §1.2. Idea 3's CAGR edge is negative, so it does not qualify.
