1. Why this paper / blog

Nowcasting is the problem of estimating a low-frequency target (e.g. quarterly GDP) while higher-frequency predictors arrive with ragged edges and missing cells. Classical Dynamic Factor Models (DFMs) fill those holes inside an EM + Kalman loop; machine-learning imputers (KNN, trees) ignore the joint distribution of whole batches.

This paper’s hook is distributional: treat imputation as choosing the missing entries so that two random batches drawn from the (completed) dataset look like they came from the same probability law, measured by a Sinkhorn divergence (entropic optimal transport). That gives a transparent, non-parametric imputer, and optionally a Sinkhorn “polish” step bolted onto EM-DFM.

We care about it when: (i) you want a paper-backed way to complete ragged panels, or (ii) you want a US GDP nowcast as a research feature — not when you need portfolio weights vs static Option A.

2. What the original work found

Setup (plain language)

  • Authors / venue: Alessandro Spelta, Paolo Pagnottoni, Nicolò Pecora — JRSS Series A, 2025 (DOI 10.1093/jrsssa/qnaf036).
  • Problem: Short-term forecast / nowcast of a low-frequency series by filling missing entries so the completed sample’s distribution is preserved.
  • Two modes:
    1. Stand-alone non-parametric OT imputer (Sinkhorn-divergence loss on batches).
    2. Parametric hybrid: Sinkhorn step as an extra stage inside the EM algorithm of a Dynamic Factor Model (state-space / Kalman style).
  • Evidence: Monte Carlo designs + empirical US GDP nowcasting; claim of gains vs KNN, random forests / regression trees, and standard EM-DFM, including missing data and modest samples.

Key claims

  • Sinkhorn divergence is a practical loss for “batches from the same dataset should match in law.”
  • Non-parametric OT imputation can beat common ML imputers on their simulations.
  • Adding Sinkhorn inside EM-DFM improves GDP nowcasts relative to classical EM-DFM baselines in their study.

Limitations (read these before trusting a port)

  • Domain: Macro mixed-frequency nowcasting, not asset allocation or cross-sectional ETF scoring.
  • Hyperparameters: Entropic regularization ε, batch size, learning rate / RMSprop, number of Sinkhorn iterations — results can move with these.
  • Compute: OT on batches is heavier than spline / mean fill; their repo uses MATLAB parfor for speed.
  • Identification: Matching distributions does not by itself guarantee causal or structural forecasts; it is a geometric imputation prior.
  • External validity: GDP nowcast wins need not transfer to equity/ETF return panels without a new walk-forward study.
  • Code maturity: Author MATLAB scripts (0 stars as of our check), research-oriented — not a packaged Python library.

3. Core math / intuition

Optimal transport in one sentence

Given two clouds of points (empirical measures μ and ν), OT asks for the cheapest way to move mass from μ to ν under a ground cost (usually squared Euclidean distance). The minimal cost is a Wasserstein distance; with entropic regularization it becomes Sinkhorn-computable.

Sinkhorn divergence (debiasing)

Plain entropic OT between a sample and itself is not zero. The Sinkhorn divergence corrects that (Feydy et al.; see also Genevay / Cuturi line of work):

S_ε(μ, ν) = OT_ε(μ, ν) − ½ OT_ε(μ, μ) − ½ OT_ε(ν, ν) where OT_ε is entropic optimal transport with regularization ε > 0.

Intuition: if μ and ν are two batches from the same completed dataset, we want S_ε(μ, ν) ≈ 0. Missing entries are free variables; we optimize them (gradient steps, often RMSprop in their code) to drive that divergence down.

Non-parametric imputer (sketch)

  1. Start from a ragged matrix X (rows = time, columns = series), with mask M of observed entries.
  2. Initialize fills for missing cells (e.g. spline / unconditional mean — their code uses preprocessing + OT refine).
  3. Repeatedly draw two mini-batches of completed rows (or windows).
  4. Compute Sinkhorn divergence between batch distributions; backprop / gradient-update only the imputed coordinates.
  5. Stop when divergence plateaus; read the low-frequency target’s filled value as the nowcast.
Observed cells stay pinned (conditional OT imputation). Their OT_imputerCONDITIONAL.m encodes that; OT_imputer.m is a simpler unconditional variant.

Parametric hybrid: Sinkhorn + EM-DFM

A Dynamic Factor Model says something like:

x_t = C f_t + e_t f_t = A f_{t−1} + u_t

with mixed frequency / missingness handled by EM + Kalman filter/smoother (classic nowcasting stack). Their twist: after (or inside) EM updates, run a Sinkhorn imputation polish so the completed panel better matches batch-wise distributions, then continue. Repo scripts funzioneNowcast*.m and EMalgorithm*.m implement the DFM/EM/Kalman pieces; Sinkhorn is the add-on loss step.

What “beats KNN / RF / EM-DFM” means here

On their Monte Carlo and US GDP exercise: lower RMSE (and related scores) for the target nowcast versus those baselines under missingness. That is a forecast accuracy claim for the low-frequency target — not a Sharpe / allocation-alpha claim.

4. What we would do on USA ETF / growth-panel data

Scope flag: Your Portfolios mandate defaults to approved USA ETF / growth-panel artifacts under /workspace/investments/. This paper’s native habitat is a macro mixed-frequency panel (GDP + monthly indicators). Applying it unchanged to ETF adjusted closes is a different scientific question. Expand scope deliberately if you want GDP nowcasts as features.

Option A — Stay faithful to the paper (macro nowcast feature)

  • Inputs: A ragged US macro panel (GDP quarterly + monthly activity/survey series). Not the 25 ETF growth block by itself.
  • Method: Reproduce non-parametric Sinkhorn imputer and/or Sinkhorn+EM-DFM on that panel.
  • Output: A GDP nowcast time series (and uncertainty if we add it) that CIO/Quant could later feed as a regime or feature into allocation strategies — separate Engineering ticket.
  • Leakage rules: At decision month t, optimize fills using information available ≤ t only; evaluate GDP release errors with real-time / vintage discipline if we go serious.
  • Nulls: No improvement in GDP nowcast RMSE vs EM-DFM and vs a simple AR after costs of complexity.

Option B — ETF-panel stress test (exploratory, not the paper’s claim)

  • Inputs: growth_panel_monthly_returns.csv / adj-close panel with artificial ragged missingness (or true listing start dates as missing).
  • Task: Impute missing ETF returns/prices with Sinkhorn vs KNN / spline; score reconstruction error and downstream effect on a frozen allocator (e.g. static Option A or vol-target).
  • Assumption: Rows are exchangeable enough for batch OT — weak for strong trends/regimes; may need rolling windows or returns not levels.
  • Nulls: OT fill does not reduce OOS allocation metrics vs simple fills; or it overfits ε.

What we would not do without a new ticket

  • Treat this as a drop-in replacement for vol-target / M3 optimize / strategy-registry weights.
  • Claim “OT alpha” in the ETF book without walk-forward evidence on your panel.

Implementation path if you greenlight a test

  1. Start from author MATLAB: AlessandroSpike/Nowcasting-with-Optimal-Transport (OT_imputer*.m, RealNonParamNEW.m, RealNowcast_ISNEW.m, Simulazioni*.m).
  2. For a Python research clone later: Sinkhorn divergence via PythonOT/POT (ot.sinkhorn / Sinkhorn divergence helpers) or GeomLoss; DFM-EM separately — Engineering ticket, not Quant production code.
  3. Teaching HTML (this file) stays the theory deliverable; any build needs an Engineering ticket with CLI, leakage tests, and research disclaimer.

5. How we would know it worked

CheckPass if…
Replication sanityOn a toy complete panel with holes punched in, OT fill RMSE ≤ KNN/spline on held-out cells under the paper’s Monte Carlo-style design.
Macro nowcast (Option A)US GDP nowcast RMSE / DA improves vs EM-DFM and AR on a locked OOS window; hyperparameters registered for multiple-testing honesty.
No leakageUnit tests: imputed values at t use data ≤ t; no same-month GDP realization in the feature set.
